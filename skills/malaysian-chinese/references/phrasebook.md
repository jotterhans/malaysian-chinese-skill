**Kena** deserves its own note: it's the Manglish passive marker for bad things happening to you. "Kena saman" (got fined), "kena scold by boss", "kena food poisoning", "confirm kena one" (you'll definitely get it). It never marks good outcomes — you don't "kena promotion".

## Kopitiam ordering grammar

Formula: `[drink][-O/-C][kau/gah dai/siew dai][peng]`

- **kopi** = coffee with condensed milk (default is sweet!)
- **-O** = black, with sugar → kopi-O
- **-C** = with evaporated milk → kopi-C (from Hainanese "C" 鲜 fresh)
- **kosong** = zero sugar → kopi-O kosong
- **kau** = extra strong → kopi kau
- **peng** = iced → kopi peng, teh-C peng
- **cham** = coffee + tea mixed
- **teh tarik** = pulled milk tea (the national drink)
- 100 plus, sky juice (= plain water, older slang), "water" often means any drink: "You want what water?"

## Example dialogues

**Making plans (Level 2):**
> A: "Weh, tonight yum cha or not?"
> B: "Can also. Where?"
> A: "Old Town lor, near your house one."
> B: "Okok, 9 like that I fetch you."

**Complaining (Level 2–3):**
> "Walao the jam just now, one hour only move two kilometer. Then reach already mana tau no parking somemore. Die lah next time I just Grab."

**Aunty at reunion dinner (Level 3):**
> "Aiyo, so big already ah! Got girlfriend or not? Your cousin hor, last year buy house liao wor. You leh? Aiya no rush lah, eat more, so thin!"

**Level 1 (light) — marketing copy:**
> "Balik kampung this CNY? Tapau our pineapple tarts for the family — confirm the aunties will approve."

## Malaysian Mandarin (华语) quick reference

When writing Chinese for a Malaysian audience:

| MY Mandarin | Standard | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 酱 / 酱子 | 这样 | like this/that — "酱也可以哦" |
| 做莫 | 为什么/干嘛 | why / what for |
| 几好下 | 挺好的 | quite good ("几+adj+下" pattern) |
| 够力 | 厉害/离谱 | intense, "that's nuts" |
| 巴刹 | 菜市场 | wet market (pasar) |
| 拜一/拜二…礼拜 | 周一/周二…周日 | days of week |
| 做工 | 上班 | go to work |
| 冲凉 | 洗澡 | shower |
| 水草 | 吸管 | drinking straw |
| 大耳窿 | 放高利贷的 | loan shark |
| 脚车 | 自行车 | bicycle |
| 罗里 | 卡车 | lorry/truck |
| 烧 | 烫 | hot (to touch) — "小心烧哦" |
| 还钱 | 买单/付钱 | pay the bill ("boss, 还钱!") |
| 塞车 | 堵车 | traffic jam |

Sentence-final particles carry over into Chinese too: 咯 (lor), 叻/嘞 (leh), 咩 (meh), 哦 (or), 啦 (lah) — "是酱的咯", "真的咩?"

## The three mixing directions

Malaysian Chinese code-switching runs in every direction. Use all three where fitting:

1. **Dialect/Malay → English (classic Manglish):**
   "Don't be so kancheong lah, sap sap sui only this thing."

2. **Dialect/English → Mandarin (rojak 华语):** slang words embedded in a Mandarin sentence —
   - "那个人很 lansi 的" (that guy is so arrogant)
   - "明天 yumcha 咯?" (yum cha tomorrow?)
   - "我现在 sibeh 累" (I'm damn tired now)
   - "Cincai 啦，我都可以" (whatever lah, anything works for me)
   - "我们 gab 钱买礼物" (let's pool money for the gift)

3. **Romanized Mandarin → English (the "wo mei you qian" register):** dropping pinyin-ish Mandarin phrases into English for comedic/relatable effect, popularized by Twitter/Facebook humor threads —
   - "Cannot go out leh, wo mei you qian" (我没有钱 — I'm broke)
   - "Ask him for help? Mei wen ti one" (没问题 — no problem)
   - "You do this somemore, wo hen ni" (我恨你 — I hate you, said merajuk-style, i.e. sulking-cute, not serious)

Direction 3 is strictly informal-social-media register (Level 3); never use it in marketing or UI copy.
